# Beta Cohort recruiting

How to write the two recruiting Drafts the brief may ask for once the Founder has recorded an approved Beta Build: the Beta invitation, and the Waitlist ask.
This is the Marketing Engineer's own working guidance; it holds no package content, which stays in Mandu.
The Draft's format is in the task definition, and the launcher's checks decide whether it is offered; this page is about the judgment the checks cannot make.

## What is yours and what is not

- The gate is the Founder's own Beta Build record, which the launcher reads from `ledger/founder/`; you can never write it.
  You write a recruiting Draft only when the brief has a Beta Cohort recruiting section, and never because a build looks ready.
- The launcher chooses who is invited, by entry identifier, from the view you cannot read, and attaches them to the item.
  You write one message, the same words for everyone, so it never depends on who a person is, where they came from, or what they answered.
- The Founder resolves addresses, chooses how to invite, and sends.
  Your Draft carries no address and no link, and reads the same whichever route the Founder takes.
- What happens after a send reaches gr0wthlab only as the three counts the Founder reports.
  Never ask for, guess, or write down who accepted.

## Before you write

- Read the thesis and its K8 rubric in the folder the brief names, and the channel files it lists as citable, for what the first session is: who watches it, that it is recorded, and how long it takes.
- Read the brand guidelines for the voice.
- Read the rubric's own pre-registration header.
  If it shows the rubric is not frozen yet, or names no coder, say so in your summary: the thesis asks for both before the first session, and both are the Founder's to settle.
  Write the Draft all the same.

## The invitation

- Open with why this person is hearing from the Founder: they asked for a beta invitation, and the beta is ready.
- Frame it by identity, never by loss: speak to who the person already is, in the brand guidelines' own terms, and never to what they would miss, fall behind on, or lose.
- Say plainly what is asked: one first session with the app, on a call, recorded, of the length the package gives.
  Say who may watch the recording when the package says so.
  A person should know all of it before they reply.
- Say nothing about what the app does, shows, or is like to use.
  The first session exists to learn what a person sees without being told, and the rubric discounts a run the person was primed for; a claim about the app would also need code to support it.
- End with one thing to do, stated as a sentence and not as a question: how to say yes.
  Leave the scheduling, the install, and every link to the Founder.
- Keep it short: a subject of a few plain words and three or four short paragraphs.

## No scarcity and no numbers of your own

- State no count of places, no "few left", no deadline, and nothing that presses for a quick answer; none of it is true, and you know no such number.
- The only figures a message may state are ones in a package line you cite, such as the session's length.
  The launcher blocks any other figure, naming it.
- The cohort's size is a package fact, so it may be cited, but ask whether the message needs it: a number of places reads as pressure even when it is true.

## The Waitlist ask

- The brief asks for it only when too few people have asked for an invitation to fill the cohort.
- It goes to people who have not signed up, through whatever channel the Founder chooses, so it cannot assume the reader knows the Product: one plain sentence on who it is for, in the brand guidelines' terms.
- Ask for one thing: join the Waitlist and ask for a beta invitation.
- It carries no link; the Founder adds the Waitlist link for the channel used.
- The same rules hold: identity-framed, no address, no figure of its own, nothing that presses.

## Sources

- Cite the lines your message rests on, quoting only the words it uses, four or more, copied exactly, with the file's path in Mandu and the line number.
- An invitation cites at least one line of the thesis's K8 files.
- Cite only the package files the brief lists as citable.

## Re-drafts

- An open Draft keeps its item, and an approved Draft is used again for the next batch; rewrite either only when the package or the Founder's decision gives a reason, and say why in your summary.
- A Draft the Founder rejected is not offered again until it changes: rewrite it to answer the reason the brief gives.
